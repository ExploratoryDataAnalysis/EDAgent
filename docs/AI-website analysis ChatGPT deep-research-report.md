# EDAgent UX Research: AI-Looking Cues, Genericness, Reader Outcomes, and Pre-Build Decisions

## Executive synthesis

The strongest finding is that **“looks AI-generated,” “looks generic,” and “works well” are not the same judgment**. They overlap, but the evidence does not support treating any isolated visual feature—rounded cards, gradients, dark mode, soft shadows, large type, sidebars, or even a familiar SaaS composition—as evidence that AI authored a site. Large-scale research found substantial web-design homogenization well before the present generative-AI era: Goree et al. analyzed more than 200,000 snapshots of more than 10,000 websites from 2003–2019 and found declining layout difference, with shared libraries, standardized color choices, mobile constraints, and changing design practice among plausible contributors. Their interviews also highlight the upside of conventions: familiar patterns can make interfaces easier to use. citeturn792734view0 That directly supports the research brief's requirement to separate perceived authorship from genericness. fileciteturn1file3

The controlled evidence that is closest to the modern “AI interface” question points in the same direction. A 2026 study had 92 computing/engineering students evaluate ten human- and AI-created prototypes without being told authorship. Pragmatic qualities such as clarity and efficiency tended to be positive, while originality and innovation were much weaker; one of the highest pragmatic scores belonged to a human prototype, and some AI prototypes were among the stronger overall designs. The study therefore supports **“conventionality can coexist with usability”**, not “AI interfaces are unusable,” and it cannot establish which visual cues cause people to identify authorship because authorship identification itself was not the measured task. It is also a student sample and prototype study rather than a study of public data-journalism websites. citeturn462978view2turn462978view3

Perceived AI authorship is especially vulnerable to **prior information**. In a 2025 CHI experiment with 911 participants, placing AI-content warning labels on social-media material significantly increased belief that the content was AI-generated; label design also changed trust in the label. The stimuli were social-media/deepfake content rather than websites, so transfer is indirect, but this is strong evidence for a methodological rule: if EDAgent asks participants whether a design “looks AI-made,” it should do so **after** comprehension, task, and quality measures and before revealing any AI provenance. citeturn792734view4 This directly validates the brief's requirement for blind authorship review. fileciteturn1file0

The recurring community notion of an “AI look” is better interpreted as a **compound cue of defaultness or incoherence** than as a forensic signature. Recent Hacker News discussion around a disclosed vibe-coded site included both “this still looks AI-generated” judgments and counterarguments that the issue was simply an inconsistent combination of panels, tabs, and other borrowed patterns; commenters also credited extensive human iteration and taste. citeturn104555view0turn104555view1 Another explicitly AI-generated project, Gen, disclosed that no HTML had been manually authored, yet its system prompt intentionally varied design by page purpose—dense and utilitarian for directories, playful for entertainment, minimal for tools—and at least one commenter preferred its attention value to average human-made sites. That is only anecdotal reception, but it is a useful counterexample to the claim that generative provenance necessarily produces one aesthetic. citeturn104555view2turn104555view3

Conversely, contemporary commercial templates independently reproduce many features now described in public discussion as “AI tells.” A current Framer SaaS template explicitly advertises spacious layouts, modern typography, rounded cards, soft blue accents, a strong hero, feature cards, pricing, FAQ, and final CTA; another uses a dark navy/electric-blue gradient hero, floating dashboard mockups, animated counters, card grids, tabs, FAQ and closing CTA. These are documented templates, not evidence of AI generation. citeturn441998search6turn441998search8 The result is an important counterfactual for EDAgent: **a human-made or template-built site can look “AI-ish,” and an AI-assisted site can be deliberately subject-specific**.

First impressions matter, but their evidentiary meaning is narrower than popular summaries imply. Lindgaard et al.'s 2006 experiments used homepage screenshots and found that participants could form repeatable visual-appeal judgments with extremely short exposures; Study 1 used 22 students viewing 100 pages at 500 ms, Study 2 used 31 students, and Study 3 used 40 participants split between 50 ms and 500 ms. The authors explicitly framed the result as visual appeal, not comprehension, trust, authorship, or long-term task success. citeturn816303view0 Tuch et al. later tested 59 participants on 120 company-homepage stimuli at 50/500/1000 ms and another 82 participants at approximately 17/33/50 ms. Lower visual complexity and higher prototypicality tended to improve beauty ratings, with complexity influencing very early judgments. Again, this was aesthetic response to corporate homepages, not evidence that a conventional civic interface is good or bad. citeturn934370view0turn934370view2

Trust and comprehension have a different evidence base. Fogg et al.'s 2001 study of more than 1,400 participants found perceived credibility associated with factors such as ease of use, expertise, trustworthiness and “real-world feel,” while amateurism reduced credibility; the authors themselves note that credibility is a perceived quality and that their method was a questionnaire drawing on cumulative web experience rather than task observation. citeturn792734view3 For EDAgent, therefore, decorative polish should not be used as a substitute for source visibility, exact scope, usable controls, or methodological specificity.

Communicating caveats does **not** appear to require hiding uncertainty to preserve trust. Van der Bles et al. ran five experiments with a combined 5,780 participants, including a BBC field experiment. Numerical uncertainty made uncertainty more apparent while producing little or no substantive loss of source trust; small negative effects were concentrated more in explicit verbal uncertainty than numerical ranges. The work concerns public facts and estimates rather than crash-record coverage, but it is unusually direct evidence against the fear that honest qualification automatically undermines credibility. citeturn462978view0turn462978view1 For EDAgent this strengthens the case for putting coverage, denominators, coded-era boundaries and missingness close to the claim they qualify rather than burying them in a methods page.

Narrative order also deserves attention independently of visual style. Hullman et al. analyzed 42 professional narrative visualizations and experimentally tested sequencing. One study involved 143 Mechanical Turk participants across 875 trials; another recruited 82 master's students, retaining 73 after a verification screen. Some sequence effects appeared in preference/memory, while hypothesized accuracy differences were not statistically significant in the second experiment. This is evidence that sequencing can matter, but not that a single “scrollytelling” order is universally superior. citeturn816303view2 The Pudding's *Life After Death on Wikipedia* supplies a useful applied example: it defines its adjusted pageview measure before asking readers to interpret it, establishes an ordinary Beyoncé baseline, then introduces the extraordinary Prince spike, expands to a larger set, and only later moves into longer-term patterns. citeturn915140view0 The transferable decision is **baseline → comparison → qualification → broader interpretation**, not “copy The Pudding's visual style.”

For EDAgent, the central release decision in the supplied audit—**make the Allegheny County investigation the public entry point while retaining a task-oriented analyser separately**—is well supported at the information-architecture level. The audit itself distinguishes the three reader goals and proposes public investigation, record, map/evidence, methods, hypothetical scenario, and analyser routes. fileciteturn1file7 fileciteturn1file9 The scientific constraints in the supplied Version 3 plan make that separation especially valuable: its research record is explicitly descriptive and evidence-bounded, keeps real site-level prediction/recommendation out of scope, and treats scenario arithmetic as hypothetical. [Supplied EDAgent Project Plan v3](sandbox:/mnt/data/EDAgent_Project_Plan_v3(1).tex)

The most important disagreement with a purely visual diagnosis of the current Overview is that the pictured problem is **not primarily an “AI vibe” problem**. According to the audit's direct inspection of `Screenshot 2026-11-13 164315.png` at 1535×722, the page displays “Reviewed data unavailable,” leaving the shell and blank area to dominate. The same audit says the image alone cannot identify which release read or verifier condition caused the failure. fileciteturn1file5 If reviewed data became available while the shell remained unchanged, the page could become substantially more useful because evidence would exist to read. If the design changed while reviewed data remained unavailable, a better pending-state explanation and methods-oriented entry could improve orientation, but not restore findings. The full public experience therefore requires **both evidence availability and editorial hierarchy**. That is a testable competing-hypothesis model, not a causal diagnosis.

The calculator has a similarly bounded diagnosis. The audit's direct screenshot inspection of `Screenshot 2026-11-13 164337.png` at 1535×862 found a reference selector and long unfilled form with no worked result visible; the untouched state provides no evidence that the calculation itself is unreliable. fileciteturn1file5 The plausible UX hypothesis is that the initial state imposes too much specification before users see what the tool does. The proposed one-click invented example is supported by general form/task principles and by comparison with tools that establish purpose before asking for inputs, but **EDAgent-specific improvement must be tested**, especially because a prominent preset could be misread as an observed county result unless its hypothetical status is unmistakable.

Finally, the analyser should not be forced into the investigation's editorial idiom. Application shells are conventional because persistent navigation, state, datasets, jobs and tools are genuinely useful in task-driven software. Homogenization research explicitly warns that similarity and usability are not opposites. citeturn792734view0 The research therefore supports **differentiation by purpose**: editorial, evidence-first public reading; a small, unmistakably hypothetical calculator; and a conventional, stateful workspace for general data analysis.

### Investigation versus investigation

**The Pudding versus the proposed EDAgent investigation.** The transferable strength is not illustration style or scrolling animation. It is that the reader encounters a **defined measure before an inference**, then a normal comparison before the exceptional observation. The Pudding defines “adjusted pageviews,” shows Beyoncé as a comprehensible baseline, then introduces Prince's post-death jump, before moving to the much larger collection. citeturn915140view0 For EDAgent, the analogous sequence is not “county total → dramatic hotspot.” Given the project's restrictions, a safer sequence is **what is in this historical crash extract → what portion supports the current comparison → where coding/coverage changes → what the descriptive pattern shows**. The audit's proposed investigation-first route is therefore well aligned with narrative evidence, provided it does not turn review-only notebook outputs into public claims. fileciteturn1file7

A reader journey worth testing is: “What records are we looking at?” → “What can I compare?” → “What changed in measurement?” → “Where is the source?” The key tradeoff is speed versus qualification. Putting every limitation before the first figure would create instruction burden; hiding all limitations until Methods would encourage overinterpretation. The uncertainty literature suggests a better middle course: concise quantitative scope/caveat beside the finding, with deeper method available on demand. citeturn462978view0turn462978view1

### Dashboard versus dashboard

**Current EDAgent Overview state versus Vision Zero View and PCIT.** In the supplied screenshot audit, EDAgent's first screen is dominated by an application shell and unavailable content. fileciteturn1file5 Vision Zero View is visually much more utilitarian than a modern editorial story, yet it performs an important interpretive job in its information panel: it distinguishes citywide totals from the subset with usable geographic information, names mapping transformations, explains highway exclusions and warns about category limitations. citeturn915140view2 PCIT similarly uses conventional navigation but makes the jobs explicit—featured report, custom query, visualization, raw database—and puts an incompleteness warning directly on YTD statistics. citeturn915140view5

That comparison weakens the claim that EDAgent needs novelty to feel trustworthy. What it needs first is **question-specific hierarchy and scope disclosure**. A conventional chart and table with an exact denominator, coverage note, date, source and download can outperform a highly customized interface that leaves provenance ambiguous.

### Documentation versus editorial design

**NHTSA FARS and OWID are counterexamples to the proposition that restrained sites are inherently generic or weak.** FARS provides a dense, conventional agency page, but its inclusion rule is exceptionally clear: it says exactly what makes a crash part of the dataset. citeturn915140view3 OWID's road-injury data page gives provider, source vintage, update date, date range, unit, processing and citation details in a highly standardized framework. citeturn915140view1 Both demonstrate that standardization can be a virtue where the recurring reader question is “what does this number mean and where did it come from?”

For EDAgent, this argues against redesigning every provenance element into a bespoke editorial device. Source/date/coverage/download components may reasonably be standardized across pages, while the **narrative ordering and annotations around them** remain subject-specific.

### Calculator versus calculator

The Global Road Safety Facility calculator establishes purpose before computation: it tells policymakers what the tool is for, what kinds of inputs it uses, the class of estimated benefit it produces and which intervention families are covered before offering two modules. citeturn915140view4 EDAgent has a stricter scientific boundary: its calculator is illustrative arithmetic, **not a real-site recommendation or estimate of treatment benefit**. The supplied v3 plan and audit make that separation explicit. fileciteturn1file7

That difference matters more than visual polish. An EDAgent worked example could be valuable precisely because it teaches the arithmetic, but it must use visibly invented values and carry its hypothetical status into the result, sensitivity range, copy/share state and any export. The CMF Clearinghouse is a complementary reference rather than a calculation model: it exposes study context and quality information behind intervention factors, reinforcing that “reference evidence” and “scenario arithmetic” should not be collapsed into one apparently authoritative recommendation. citeturn441998search2turn441998search5

### Template versus AI-assisted counterexamples

The Framer template example is useful because its documented features strongly resemble motifs criticized in AI-sameness discussions: dark gradient hero, dashboard mockups, floating cards, counters, repeated blocks and CTA architecture. citeturn441998search8 That establishes a precondition for responsible interpretation: seeing those motifs on EDAgent could support a judgment of **generic technology-site styling**, but not of AI authorship.

The Railcode discussion supplies the opposite boundary. Even when AI assistance was disclosed, commenters debated whether remaining “AI-looking” features reflected AI or simply inconsistent design choices; other comments emphasized that human taste and iteration were what made the result feel deliberate. citeturn104555view0turn104555view1 Gen goes further: its disclosed AI-generation prompt deliberately asks for different visual systems depending on the requested content type. citeturn104555view2turn104555view3 Together these cases support a more useful EDAgent research question: **“Does every design decision appear to be serving this reader, subject and state?”** rather than “Can we eliminate visual motifs associated with AI?”

The competing hypotheses should remain separate:

- **Data-availability hypothesis.** If the reviewed data load while the present shell stays unchanged, readers gain the actual charts/tables and therefore may become able to complete evidence tasks. This likely improves utility dramatically, but is unobserved.
- **Editorial-design hypothesis.** If the data remain unavailable but the page becomes a useful public document—scope, methods, evidence-status explanation, what will be available after review, and routes to the hypothetical calculator/analyser—the dead-end experience can improve, but no substantive finding is restored.
- **Combined hypothesis.** With reviewed data available *and* public-document hierarchy, the page can expose a bounded question, first evidence, coverage/caveat and provenance before secondary navigation. This is the state most consistent with the external evidence, but it still requires reader validation.

**Useful patterns to preserve:** explicit unavailable-state wording rather than silent fallback; a retry path; direct route to the illustrative tool; existing separation between public road-safety routes and analyser functionality described in the audit. The release gate itself is not UX debt to be “designed around”; it is a scientific control. fileciteturn1file7

**Audit proposals that are well supported:** make the investigation the public entry point; expose source/method/coverage near claims; split public-reading layout from analyser workspace; preserve explicit unavailable states; make Methods useful even when gated evidence is unavailable. These align with NHTSA/OWID/Vision Zero provenance patterns, uncertainty evidence and the distinct purposes of editorial and workspace interfaces. citeturn915140view1turn915140view2turn915140view3turn462978view1

**Plausible but untested:** the audit's proposed warm paper palette, serif/sans pairing, subject illustration, exact 12-column composition, and removal of specific card treatments. Those may reduce perceived genericness and improve subject identity, but no EDAgent reader evidence yet shows that they improve comprehension or trust. They should be treated as art-direction hypotheses, not research conclusions.

**In tension with the evidence:** any redesign whose objective is primarily to “not look AI-generated,” particularly if it sacrifices familiar controls, fast loading, accessible tables or explicit status information. The evidence says genericness can predate AI and that conventionality can coexist with usability. citeturn792734view0turn462978view3

The first-screen outcome to protect is not “wow.” It is: **within one viewport, can the reader tell what this record is, whether reviewed evidence is currently available, what the first legitimate question is, and where to verify scope?**

### Illustrative scenario calculator

**Supported issue:** the supplied screenshot audit says `Screenshot 2026-11-13 164337.png` (1535×862) shows a reference selector and long unfilled form, with no worked result visible. fileciteturn1file5 That establishes entry-state burden in the literal sense that many controls precede a demonstrated outcome; it does not establish that users are confused or that a preset will improve performance.

The strongest research hypothesis is **output discovery before parameter mastery**. A first-time reader may understand a hypothetical tool more quickly after seeing a visibly invented example, result and formula, then editing values, than after being asked to configure every parameter before seeing the consequence. The Global Road Safety Facility calculator provides an applied comparison by stating purpose, inputs and output class before directing users into modules. citeturn915140view4 This is not evidence that EDAgent should mimic its real-world benefit-estimation logic; EDAgent's project plan expressly forbids that interpretation.

**Useful patterns to preserve:** explicit parameter fields; separate evidence/reference context; deterministic calculation; sensitivity controls; refusal of real-site recommendation semantics described in the audit. fileciteturn1file7

**Well-supported proposal:** make “hypothetical / illustrative” visible at input and result, not only in explanatory prose; preserve assumptions and formula in the result state. This follows the project's scientific boundary and provenance logic.

**Plausible but untested proposal:** a one-click invented scenario that populates inputs and immediately displays a result. Its effect should be tested on time to first valid result, error rate and ability to state “this is hypothetical, not an observed or recommended site treatment.” It should not use county-like numbers likely to be mistaken for actual EDAgent findings.

**Potential tension:** placing external treatment references immediately beside a hypothetical factor could unintentionally make the factor look endorsed or empirically transferred to Allegheny County. FHWA's CMF material stresses study context, statistical properties and appropriate selection; a factor's existence is not proof of local applicability. citeturn441998search2turn441998search5

### General dataset analyser

The analyser is the experience for which **an application shell is most defensible**. A dataset workspace benefits from durable navigation, current dataset/session identity, analysis/history/export affordances and stateful tools. The fact that these patterns are conventional does not make them poor design; longitudinal design research explicitly notes that shared patterns can improve familiarity and usability. citeturn792734view0

The audit says the current public chrome includes “Sign in to analyser,” while the root analyser workflow itself is authentication-oriented, and proposes preserving the existing engine while differentiating public reading routes. fileciteturn1file7 That separation is well supported. The analyser should not inherit a narrative-investigation layout just to make the whole product visually uniform.

**Useful patterns to preserve:** persistent task navigation, deterministic tools, stateful workspace patterns, clear account/persistence boundary, and explicit provider-failure handling described in the audit. AI reliability should remain a separate product question unless it affects the navigation/task being tested. That matches the user's brief. fileciteturn1file1

**Well-supported proposal:** make the transition explicit—e.g., public investigation as evidence-reading; analyser as “work with a dataset”—so users do not mistake generated analysis for approved road-safety evidence. A named boundary is more consequential than whether both surfaces share the same card radius.

**Plausible but untested:** guest sample access, alternative public-to-workspace CTA wording, and exact account placement. Those involve security, persistence and product strategy beyond this visual research and need separate testing.

**High-priority accessibility/performance requirements:** keyboard navigation, visible focus, reflow, understandable error/status states and acceptable interaction latency. WCAG explicitly requires keyboard and focus behavior, and Core Web Vitals give field measures for responsiveness/load/stability. citeturn419548search4turn419548search5turn419548search8 The analyser is the surface where a “distinctive” redesign is most likely to hurt users if it replaces tested workspace conventions with editorial motion.

Across all three experiences, the audit recommendation should therefore be carried forward with one adjustment in emphasis: **investigation-first is supported because it matches reader purpose and scientific scope, not because the existing shell “looks AI-generated.”** The shell's genericness is a secondary issue; evidence availability, hierarchy, scope, provenance, hypothetical/observed separation and task completion are the primary ones.

## Pre-build reader validation

A feasible pre-build study can answer the major open questions without requiring a production redesign. Recruit **12–16 participants** across the actual intended audience rather than design professionals: roughly one-third general county/public readers, one-third planners/researchers or civic-data users, and one-third analytically confident users who could plausibly use the analyser. Include at least several keyboard-only or assistive-technology users in either this round or a dedicated accessibility round; public-service accessibility guidance recommends testing with people who use assistive technology rather than relying only on automated checks. citeturn419548search21 This is a formative sample, not a population estimate.

Use a within-/between-subject hybrid so visual-only effects can be separated from substantive content effects. Every substantive road-safety statement in the stimuli should either come from **approved public evidence** or be neutral methodological text; do not insert frozen notebook counts merely to make a prototype look complete. Research-only numeric examples in the calculator should be conspicuously labeled invented/hypothetical.

| Test block | Stimuli | Primary tasks and measures | What it can establish | What it cannot establish |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Overview: visual-only** | Current unavailable-state content versus an alternative presentation containing the *same words, same absence of reviewed data, same links* but different hierarchy/chrome | “What is available now?” “Where would you verify scope?” Time, success, wrong inference, perceived clarity, visual quality | Whether presentation alone improves orientation/genericness judgments | Whether the populated investigation works; effect of substantive content |
| **Overview: substantive** | Same visual system, one version with current sparse unavailable copy; one with concise scope/status/method context and clear next action | Identify what is known, unavailable, and where source/method lives | Value of editorial context independent of visual art direction | Effect of actual approved findings |
| **Populated investigation fixture** | Only after an approved release fixture exists: identical reviewed data/copy with two narrative orders | State main finding **with caveat**, identify numerator/denominator or coverage basis, locate source, recognize coded-era break | Comprehension, provenance discovery, recall, sequence effect | Long-term learning or general public impact |
| **Calculator entry** | Current long-form entry versus same fields plus a one-click visibly invented preset/result | Get to first valid result; explain formula; state whether output is observed, predicted, recommended, or hypothetical; find assumptions/reference | Effect of worked example on task completion and epistemic boundary | Real intervention validity or policy usefulness |
| **Analyser transition** | Public investigation with two alternative transitions to analyser; analyser shell itself held constant | “You have another dataset: where do you go?” “How do you return to the public evidence?” | Discoverability and conceptual separation | AI analysis reliability |
| **Authorship judgment** | Same stimuli, with no provenance cue during all earlier tasks | Only after task/quality ratings: “How do you think this was produced?” “What cues made you think that?” confidence, free response | Which cues this audience associates with AI/template/human design and whether those judgments correlate with task quality | Actual authorship from aesthetics |
| **Disclosure counterfactual** | Optional final randomized disclosure: “AI-assisted,” “human-designed,” or no statement applied to an otherwise identical stimulus | Re-rate authorship confidence and trust/quality | Whether production labels bias judgment for this site | General label effects outside the tested wording |

The sequence is critical. First ask factual/task questions; then ask comprehension and trust; then aesthetic/genericness judgments; **only then ask perceived authorship**. The label experiment shows why: disclosure itself can move beliefs about whether something was AI-created. citeturn792734view4

For the investigation, success should be operational rather than impressionistic: a participant can state the principal reviewed finding **and its limiting caveat**, locate the source/method, identify the relevant period or denominator, recognize when missingness changes interpretation, and refrain from converting a historical concentration into a risk or causal claim. For the unavailable state, success means correctly saying that reviewed public evidence is unavailable rather than treating the absence as “zero” or as proof that analysis does not exist.

For the calculator, the most important outcome is **calibrated interpretation**. A successful participant can run the example, explain in plain language what the arithmetic did, and say that the result is a hypothetical scenario rather than an observed county effect, site recommendation or forecast. Record time to first meaningful result, number of field errors, use of reset/example, whether references are mistaken for recommended factors, and whether the participant can recover after intentionally invalid input.

For the analyser, measure task completion independently of visual originality: can a participant identify that this is a separate dataset workspace, find the correct entry point, understand whether sign-in is needed, locate a deterministic analysis function, and return to public evidence? The research question is whether a conventional shell helps this task, not whether the shell resembles other software.

After each task, capture confidence in the answer and compare it with correctness. That gives a simple measure of **calibration**: high confidence in a wrong causal/site-risk inference is worse than modest confidence in a correct scoped interpretation. For source verification, record not merely whether a source link exists but whether participants can find the relevant source and explain what it establishes.

Use short delayed recall near the end of the session: without revisiting the page, ask participants to restate the finding, the key caveat, and whether the calculator output was real or hypothetical. Narrative-sequencing research gives reason to measure memory separately from immediate accuracy; the two do not necessarily move together. citeturn816303view2

For accessibility, test 200% text zoom/reflow, keyboard-only route completion, visible focus, chart/table alternatives, status announcements after calculation, form-error recovery and mobile reading order. WCAG 2.2 specifically addresses reflow, keyboard operation and focus visibility. citeturn419548search0turn419548search4turn419548search5 Do not treat “passes an automated accessibility checker” as evidence that the scientific story is understandable.

Pre-register the key hypotheses informally before sessions so aesthetic preferences do not drive interpretation afterward:

**H1:** Visual restyling of the unavailable Overview will change aesthetic/genericness ratings more than evidence comprehension.

**H2:** Adding substantive scope/provenance context while holding visual style fixed will improve correct interpretation and source finding more than visual restyling alone.

**H3:** Once reviewed data are available, putting the claim, denominator/coverage caveat and source in the same reading sequence will improve “finding + caveat” recall relative to separating the caveat into Methods.

**H4:** A one-click, unmistakably hypothetical calculator example will reduce time to first valid result and improve understanding of the calculator's purpose without increasing false belief that the output is a county finding.

**H5:** The analyser's conventional application shell will support workspace-task completion better than an editorialized shell, even if participants rate it as less distinctive.

**H6:** Perceived AI authorship will correlate more strongly with clusters of familiar defaults, generic copy and aesthetic incoherence than with objective task success; authorship guesses will be partly changed by disclosure.

These tests would produce evidence about EDAgent readers. They would **not** establish that one style is universally “human-looking,” that AI assistance caused any defect, or that passing a comprehension test validates the scientific claims themselves.

## Sources, search log, and unresolved questions

### Annotated bibliography

**Goree, Doosti, Crandall & Su, CHI 2021 — *Investigating the Homogenization of Web Design*.** Mixed-methods analysis of over 200,000 historical snapshots from more than 10,000 sites plus 11 experienced designer interviews. The strongest source for the proposition that visual sameness predates current GenAI and is associated with frameworks, code overlap, standardization and responsive constraints. Transfer limitation: primarily American/English web samples and computational similarity measures, not public-road-safety comprehension. citeturn792734view0

**Romero et al., 2026 — *Usable but Conventional*.** Ninety-two computing/engineering students rated ten human and AI-generated prototypes without being told authorship using UEQ-S. Pragmatic quality was generally stronger than originality/innovation. Directly relevant to separating usable from distinctive, but not a production-website or authorship-detection experiment; the current source is a recent preprint. citeturn462978view2turn462978view3

**Gamage et al., CHI 2025 — AI warning-label study.** Randomized 911 participants across label designs/control on social-media content. Presence of labels significantly increased belief that content was AI-generated. Strong evidence that prior disclosure can contaminate perceived-authorship measurement; domain transfer to website design remains indirect. citeturn792734view4

**Lindgaard et al., 2006 — first impression in 50 ms.** Three studies using homepage screenshots; 22 participants in the first, 31 in the second, and 40 in the third. Supports fast visual-appeal formation, not the popular overgeneralization that users determine a site's overall quality, trustworthiness or authorship in 50 ms. citeturn816303view0

**Tuch et al., 2012 — visual complexity and prototypicality.** Study 1 used 59 participants and 120 selected company-homepage screenshots; Study 2 used 82 participants and shorter exposures. Lower complexity and greater prototypicality improved beauty judgments. Important boundary: conventionality can improve first impressions, so “make it unusual” is not an evidence-based universal remedy for genericness. citeturn934370view0turn934370view2

**Fogg et al., CHI 2001 — website credibility.** More than 1,400 participants rated 51 web-site elements via questionnaire. Credibility factors included ease of use, expertise, trustworthiness and real-world feel; amateurism and commercial implications reduced credibility. Useful for trust hypotheses, but it is self-report and historically early web research rather than a modern behavioral test. citeturn792734view3

**van der Bles et al., PNAS 2020 — uncertainty and public trust.** Five experiments, total N=5,780, including a BBC News field experiment. Numerical uncertainty increased perceived uncertainty with little source-trust penalty. Strong evidence for visible quantitative qualification; transfer from estimated national statistics to local administrative crash-record coverage is not exact. citeturn462978view0turn462978view1

**Hullman et al., TVCG 2013 — narrative visualization sequence.** Qualitative analysis of 42 professional visualizations plus user studies, including 143 MTurk participants in one study and 82 master's students in another. Finds that sequence can influence subjective experience and memory, but not every hypothesized accuracy result was significant. Useful antidote to simplistic “scrollytelling always improves understanding” claims. citeturn816303view2

**The Pudding — *Life After Death on Wikipedia*, 2018.** Applied editorial reference. Defines its transformed measure before use, establishes a concrete baseline, introduces a striking comparison, then broadens scope and qualifications. Good example of subject-specific narrative ordering; no independent reader-outcome experiment was found for this article. citeturn915140view0

**Our World in Data — road-injury data page.** Applied provenance reference. Shows provider, definition, update date, period, unit, processing, citation and licensing in a repeatable standardized structure. Demonstrates that a conventional framework can be highly specific about evidence. citeturn915140view1

**NYC Vision Zero View.** Applied civic-dashboard reference. Explicitly distinguishes all totals from mappable subsets and names mapping exclusions and category limitations. Especially relevant to EDAgent's need to separate overall records, records with usable coordinates, and mapped aggregate outputs. citeturn915140view2

**NHTSA FARS.** Restrained federal documentation reference. Leads with an inclusion rule precise enough to determine what a record represents. Useful for the “what entered this evidence base?” question; FARS definitions must not be imported into the Allegheny dataset. citeturn915140view3

**Pennsylvania Crash Information Tool.** Closest state-level public crash-data comparator located. Separates canned reports, custom query, dashboards and raw downloads, while warning that YTD data are incomplete and can change. Applied observation, not measured reader-comprehension evidence. citeturn915140view5

**FHWA CMF Clearinghouse.** Intervention-evidence reference. Records study design, sample, statistical properties and source for CMFs and provides guidance on selecting appropriate factors. Useful as a provenance model; it does not make EDAgent's illustrative factor a locally valid treatment estimate. citeturn441998search2turn441998search5

**W3C WCAG 2.2 and public-service accessibility guidance.** Normative/practice evidence for keyboard interaction, reflow, focus and recoverable errors. These are acceptance requirements and testing frameworks, not evidence that a particular EDAgent narrative is understandable. citeturn419548search0turn419548search4turn419548search21

### Unresolved research and access questions

The most consequential unresolved question is **what the approved, populated Overview actually looks and behaves like**. The supplied audit correctly warns that an empty desktop screenshot cannot establish the populated experience. fileciteturn1file1 A future evidence pass needs a screenshot or accessible rendered state of the same revision with reviewed data available, plus loading, partial network failure, approval revocation, keyboard, 390-pixel/mobile and print/table states.

The second gap is the repository re-verification. The audit's inspected commit is precise and should remain the implementation anchor, but this research session did not independently retrieve `87a7aea1d10300151bafa33a21a1735d4a011e29`. Before implementation decisions are converted into tickets, the team should re-open that exact revision and verify whether `RoadSafetyPage.tsx`, `InterventionPage.tsx`, layout routing and release-service behavior remain unchanged from the audit. Until then, those are **audit/source claims**, not independent rendered observations.

The third gap is direct evidence about **website-specific AI-authorship attribution**. Controlled research located here either studies labels/content or user experience of known human/AI prototypes; community discussions supply rich cues but poor causal identification. Accordingly, no “AI marker checklist” should be treated as scientifically diagnostic. EDAgent's blind post-task authorship test is the appropriate way to create local evidence.

The fourth gap is the calculator's completed state. The screenshot only shows idle input. A filled, calculated, sensitivity, error, reset and narrow-screen state should be inspected before concluding that result hierarchy is defective. The same applies to the analyser: this report supports the *type* of app-shell interaction, but lacks a complete authenticated session recording and therefore does not evaluate its end-to-end AI or analysis reliability.

The fifth gap is measured reader response to the external comparators. Most live-site comparisons in this report are **independent site observations**, not experiments on their audiences. OWID, NHTSA, Vision Zero View, PCIT and The Pudding show design decisions worth testing; they do not prove those decisions will improve EDAgent. The strongest causal evidence remains the controlled research on immediate visual judgments, credibility perceptions, uncertainty communication and narrative sequencing, each with transfer limitations described above.

The release boundary remains non-negotiable throughout that future work. Exact bytes matching a known candidate are not publication approval; a genuine human release decision remains required. Historical county or grid counts cannot be promoted into site-specific risk, exposure-normalized rate, causal effect, prediction or treatment benefit. Source blanks, encoded unknowns, unusable geography, coverage denominators and coded-era changes must remain distinct. Hypothetical calculator values must stay visibly hypothetical. The analyser's AI reliability remains a separate product-validation problem. Those constraints come from the supplied project audit and Version 3 plan, and should outrank any aesthetic objective. fileciteturn1file6turn1file7 [Supplied Project Plan v3](sandbox:/mnt/data/EDAgent_Project_Plan_v3(1).tex)
